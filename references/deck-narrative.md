# Deck Narrative Canon

> Storytelling brain for `pitch-narrative`. Templates + the validated slide spine.
> The 14-slide type-correct spine is adopted from the MIT-licensed FluidDocs
> deck-builder and the MIT zolidar pitch-builder (audited against decks that
> closed rounds). Other templates are public frameworks, summarized.

## The validated 14-slide spine (type-correct backbone)

Order is a default, not dogma — apply the traction-first rule below.

1. **Title** — company + one-line purpose (the category anchor).
2. **Problem** — who bleeds, how often, how expensively.
3. **Solution** — the aha; outcome, not feature list.
4. **Why now** — the named recent shift.
5. **Market / TAM** — bottom-up sizing.
6. **Product** — show it working.
7. **Traction** — proof of pull.
8. **Business model** — who pays what, why.
9. **Competition / moat** — honest matrix + defensibility.
10. **Go-to-market** — repeatable path to customers.
11. **Team** — founder-market fit.
12. **Financials** — 3-yr realistic + unit economics.
13. **The ask** — exact $, use of funds, milestones.
14. **Vision** — the world if you win (the dinner-test line).

*(Appendix: detailed cohorts, security, references — proof on tap, not in the spine.)*

## Traction-first rule

If early metrics are exceptional (real revenue, compound growth, marquee
customers), move **Traction** to slide 2–3. VCs scan 30–60s; lead with the proof
that makes them keep reading. Otherwise lead with Problem.

## Headline rule (the spine test)

Reading ONLY the headlines must reconstruct the full investment thesis. Each
headline is a **complete assertion sentence** (not a label), max 2 lines, insight
front-loaded. "Market is a $4B bottom-up opportunity" — not "Market Size".

## Template library (pick the spine that fits the story)

- **Sequoia (10 slides)** — Company purpose · Problem · Solution · Why now · Market size · Competition · Product · Business model · Team · Financials. The systematic default.
- **YC standard** — one-liner · problem · insight · what you do · why now · market · traction · team · ask. Terse, traction-forward.
- **Kawasaki 10/20/30** — ≤10 slides, ≤20 minutes, ≥30pt font. A discipline constraint, not a sequence.
- **Andy Raskin 5-step strategic narrative** — (1) name the big shift/enemy, (2) show the promised land, (3) introduce features as "magic gifts" for reaching it, (4) evidence you can deliver, (5) the stakes. Use to frame Why-now → Solution → Proof.
- **Duarte sparkline** — oscillate "what is" vs "what could be", end on the new bliss. Use to keep tension across the arc.

## Grounding lenses

- **Khosla (emotional)** — a pitch must make the listener *feel* the inevitability and the stakes; lead with the human, not the spec.
- **Sequoia (systematic)** — but every emotional beat must be backed by a number or a demo. Emotion opens the door; evidence closes it.
- Use both: Khosla picks what to say first, Sequoia ensures it's defensible.

## Why-now + demo craft

- **Why now** = the single most under-used slide. Pin the exact tech/regulatory/behavioral shift from the last 1–3 years. No inevitability → the VC assumes incumbents will do it.
- **Demo/product** = show the real artifact (screens, a flow, a short clip). Abstract architecture diagrams persuade no one.

## Hand-off to pitch-draft

`pitch-narrative` outputs `narrative-plan.md`: the chosen spine + the ordered list
of **headline assertions** (one per slide) that alone reconstruct the thesis. Draft
fills each headline with ≤5 bullets of assertion→evidence.
