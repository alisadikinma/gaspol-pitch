# VC Fundamentals — how investors think

> Brain for `pitch-discovery` (scoping the gap list to the raise stage) and
> `pitch-review` (DD / objection pass). Public venture knowledge, summarized.

## The power law (why VCs ask what they ask)

VC returns follow a power law: a few investments return the whole fund. A VC isn't
asking "will this work?" — they're asking **"could this be the fund-returner?"**.
That's why they push on market size, moat, and venture-scale growth. A solid
business with a small ceiling is a pass, not a compliment.

- **Ownership × outcome** — a fund needs each "win" to return a meaningful % of the fund. Ownership target (often ~10–20%) × a believable exit must clear that bar.
- **Implication for the deck:** show the path to a very large outcome, credibly. "Nice business" loses to "could be enormous, here's why".

## Stage map (what each stage must SHOW)

Raise/valuation bands are **rules of thumb — verify against current market**.

| Stage | Typical raise | What the investor must see | What's OK to be missing |
|-------|--------------|----------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Pre-seed** | ~$250K–$1M | Founder-market fit, a sharp insight, an early prototype, a wedge | Revenue, full team |
| **Seed** | ~$1M–$4M | Early traction / pull, a credible wedge → big market, initial unit-econ signal | Scaled GTM, profitability |
| **Series A** | ~$5M–$15M | Repeatable GTM, real revenue + retention, LTV:CAC working, a plan to scale | Full market dominance |
| **Series B+** | $15M+ | Efficient growth (burn multiple, Rule of 40), expanding NRR, durable moat | — |

`pitch-discovery` uses this to build the **gap list**: flag only what *this stage's*
investors must see and the deck currently lacks. Don't demand Series-A proof from a
pre-seed deck.

## Instruments & dilution

- **SAFE** — most common early instrument. Key terms: **valuation cap**, **discount**, **MFN** (most-favored-nation). Uncapped + no-discount is the most founder-friendly; a cap is the practical valuation.
- **Priced round** — actual equity at a set pre-money valuation; triggers a full term sheet.
- **Dilution math** — new ownership = raise ÷ post-money. Stack rounds + option pool to see founder ownership over time. A common pre-seed→A path leaves founders meaningfully diluted; plan the pool.
- **Option pool** — investors often want it carved from pre-money (dilutes founders, not them) — know this in the negotiation.

## Term-sheet key terms (know these cold)

- **Liquidation preference** — who gets paid first on exit (1x non-participating is standard/clean).
- **Pro-rata rights** — investor's right to maintain ownership in later rounds.
- **Board composition** — who controls seats post-round.
- **Option pool** — size + whether pre- or post-money.
- **Anti-dilution** — protection if a later round prices lower (broad-based weighted-average is normal).

## Due-diligence checklist (what they'll verify after the meeting)

- Traction numbers reproducible (not cumulative vanity).
- Cap table clean; no dead-equity founders, no IP-ownership ambiguity.
- Customer references / retention real.
- Market sizing defensible bottom-up.
- Unit economics hold under scrutiny.
- Team references + founder-market-fit checks out.

## Top investor objections (and how a deck pre-empts each)

| Objection | Pre-empt with |
|-----------|---------------|
| "Market's too small" | Bottom-up TAM + expansion path (adjacent segments) |
| "No moat / easy to copy" | Proprietary data / network effect / switching cost, named |
| "Why now / why hasn't this been done" | A specific recent shift on the Why-now slide |
| "Can this team execute" | Founder-market-fit one-liner + relevant track record |
| "Traction looks thin" | Compound growth + retention cohort, not totals |
| "Unit economics don't work" | LTV:CAC ≥ 3×, payback < 12 mo, COGS acknowledged |
| "Crowded space" | Honest competitor matrix + the one axis you own |

`pitch-review` runs this list as an objection pass: for each, is the deck's answer
present and convincing? A missing answer is a weak spot to surface.
